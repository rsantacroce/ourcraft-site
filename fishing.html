An Ourcraft mod you make yourself · 8 steps

Fishing Fun

Make a mod that fills Ourcraft with fish you draw yourself, secret fishing spots, magic rods, a fish market and a big feast. You'll write it in a computer language called EELisp, one small step at a time.

New! Mods in EELisp are brand new. If your .el file doesn't show on the Mods screen, your Ourcraft is too old for them: update it first. Grown-ups who want to make mods in Rust can read the modding guide.

  • 7 fish you draw with letters, from the Puddle Perch to the rare Rainbow Fish
  • 5 places to fish: ponds, deep water, caves, snow, and night
  • 2 rods to craft: the Lucky Rod and the Golden Rod
  • A fish market to sell your catch for Fish Coins
  • Cooking, a Fish Sandwich and a Fisher's Feast
  • A journal that counts the fish you've found

For a grown-up: getting ready

About 5 minutes. After that, the child can do the rest.

  1. A text editor that saves plain text. Visual Studio Code is free and colours matching brackets. Notepad works on Windows. On a Mac, TextEdit works after Format → Make Plain Text.
  2. The mods folder. In Ourcraft, click Play, then the Mods button next to a world. That screen shows where the folder is (on a Mac, ~/Library/Application Support/Ourcraft/mods). Make a file there called fishing.el. The name stays the same for the whole tutorial.
  3. A world. Make a new Creative world. Its inventory (E) has every item, including the fishing rod and everything the mod adds. Later, a Survival world is better for crafting and selling.
  4. Switch the mod on. Click Mods next to the world and click Fishing until it says on.

To try a change: save the file, press Esc → Save and quit to title, then Play the world again. The game reads the mod every time a world opens.

What's going on?

In EELisp everything goes in brackets. The first word says what to do, and the rest are what to do it with. (tell player "Soon you'll catch fish here!") means "tell this player this message".

The brackets on this page are coloured in pairs, so you can see which ones belong together.

defn defines something the game can use. The game uses on-join every time a player joins, and on-chat every time someone chats. (str "Hello, " name "!") sticks words together. The true at the end tells the game "this was my command", so nobody else sees it.

Make it yours

Change the hello message. Add a second command, /hi, that says something funny.

2Draw a fish

Now a fish you can hold. You draw it with letters! Each letter is a colour, and a dot is see-through. Try it here: change the letters and the colours, and watch the fish change.

Up to 16 rows of up to 16 letters.

Then you tell the game about your new item with register-item, like the code under the drawing pad. The curly brackets { } hold settings. Each setting has a name starting with :, then its value:

  • :name is the item's secret name in the game. It starts with fish: and has no spaces.
  • :display is the name players see.
  • :colors says what colour each letter is: three numbers [red green blue], each from 0 to 255. [255 0 0] is red, [0 0 0] is black.
  • :food 2 means you can eat it, and it fills 2 points of hunger.

3Catch it, then cook it

Ourcraft already has fishing: hold a Fishing Rod, point at water, right-click, and wait. Your mod decides what bites:

The last thing in on-fish-bite is what the player catches. (x y z say where the bobber is. You'll use them in step 5.)

To cook it, add :cooks-into "fish:cooked_perch" to the perch, and register a cooked perch with the same shape in browns and :food 6.

4Lots of fish

Let's make a list of fish. Each fish has its settings in { }, and the whole list is in [ ]. :weight says how often it bites: weight 10 bites ten times as often as weight 1, so the Rainbow Fish is rare.

A loop registers every fish: (for-each f fish-list ...) means "for each fish in the list, call it f, and do this". (dict-get f :body) reads that fish's :body setting.

The bite picks a fish, and now and then something funny:

  • (chance 8) is true 8 times out of 100.
  • (if test this that) does this if the test is true, or that if it isn't.
  • (let (f ...) ...) gives something a name for a little while. Here f is the fish that bit.

5Places to fish

Each fish gets a :place: "pond", "deep", "cave", "snow" or "night". The mod finds out where the bobber is by looking at the blocks around it with (get-block x y z):

  • y goes up: (- y 1) is one block below, (+ y 1) one above.
  • A name ending in ?, like deep?, is a question. The answer is true or false.
  • cond asks its questions in order and gives the first answer that's true. The last one, true, catches everything else.
The whole step 5 file

Try it

Fish in a pond, the sea, a cave and at night. Type /time night to make it night straight away.

Make it yours

Invent a new place: a "jungle" near core:leaves blocks, or a "high" place when y is above 100.

6Better rods

Any item whose name ends in _rod works as a fishing rod. :stack 1 means one per slot, like real tools. A recipe lets you craft it: the pattern is the 3 by 3 crafting grid, and each letter is an item.

on-fish-bite is told which rod you used, so rods can be different. The Lucky Rod makes rare fish 3 times as likely, and only the Golden Rod catches the Rainbow Fish.

The whole step 6 file

Make it yours

7The fish market

Each fish gets a :price, and there's a new item: the Fish Coin. Coins are real items, so they're saved with your world. /market builds a stall next to you. Standing next to it:

  • /sell sells every fish you carry. Cooked fish sell for double!
  • /shop shows what the market sells.
  • /buy 1 buys thing number 1 (the Golden Rod, for 30 coins).

The shop is a list, so it's easy to add to. Your coins show in the corner of the screen, with set-hud.

The whole step 7 file

Try it

In a Survival world: catch fish, cook some, build a market, sell, and save up for the Golden Rod.

Make it yours

Sell more things in the shop. Make the Rainbow Fish worth 100 coins. Build a bigger stall.

When something goes wrong

Everyone's code goes wrong sometimes, even grown-up programmers'. When your mod has a problem, the game says so in the chat, starting with (!). The script has a problem means the game couldn't read your file: it stops at the first problem, so anything after it is missing too. (on-chat …) failed means something went wrong inside on-chat.

What you seeWhat to check
unclosed listA (, [ or { is missing its partner. Count them!
expected function, got numberA list in round brackets, like (255 0 0). Lists use square ones: [255 0 0].
the picture uses the letter z…A letter in :picture that isn't in :colors.
there's already something with that nameTwo items with the same :name.
Undefined symbol: …A misspelled name, or something used before its def.
there's no block called …Block names start with core:, like core:stone.
Nothing happensIs the mod on for this world? Did you save, then Play the world again?

Cheat sheet

The game calls these in your mod

(defn on-join (player name) …)a player joins
(defn on-chat (player text) …)someone chats (give back true for your command)
(defn on-fish-bite (player rod x y z) …)a fish bites (give back what they catch)
(defn on-block-change (player x y z old new) …)a block is broken or placed (give back :deny to stop it)
(defn on-tick (tick) …)20 times a second

Your mod can do these

(tell player "hi") · (chat "hi")a message to one player · to everyone
(show-title "Wow!" 2 player)big text for 2 seconds
(set-hud player ["line 1" "line 2"])the panel in the corner
(give-item player "fish:perch" 3)give items
(take-item player "fish:perch" 3)take up to 3, and say how many it took
(count-item player "fish:coin")how many they have
(register-item {…}) · (register-block {…}) · (register-recipe {…})new things (only at the top of the file)
(get-block x y z) · (set-block x y z "core:stone")look at · change one block
(fill x1 y1 z1 x2 y2 z2 "core:glass")a whole box of blocks
(player-pos player)where they are, as [x y z]
(random 6) · (chance 25) · (pick ["a" "b"])0 to 5 · true 25 times in 100 · one of them
(confetti x y z) · (hearts x y z) · (sound "pop")a little celebration
(time-of-day)0 is midnight, 0.5 is noon

The language

(def name value)give something a name
(defn name (inputs) …)make a command of your own
(let (a 1 b 2) …)names for a little while
(if test this that) · (when test …) · (cond …)choosing
(for-each x list …)do something for each thing in a list
(+ 1 2) (- 5 1) (* 2 3) (/ 10 2)maths
(= a b) (< a b) (and …) (or …) (not …)questions
(str "a" 1 "b")stick words together
[1 2 3] · (nth list 0)a list · its first thing (counting starts at 0!)
{:a 1} · (dict-get d :a)settings · reading one
